1.1 Convolutions from Scratch

So I only made the two loop version cause I didn't know it was a requirement to make the four loop and compare the times (read the deliverables before working on the project!) So I only have my two loop version. But it basically loops through every pixel in the image, and centered around that pixel cuts a portion of the image out. Once I have that portion I flatten both that and the kernel, and then set that pixel's value to the dot product between the two. This basically takes the dot product between the two matrices. Of course, I take the image portion relative to the kernel size.

Regarding borders, for the 'full' setting I add zeros on the image I'm convoluting with relative to the kernel. So if my kernel is 3x3, I add both 3 pixels on the top and Bottom and 3 pixels on the sides of the image. For the 'same' setting I just add half of that on all edges. I flip the filter because otherwise it would just be a cross correlation and not actually a convolution, and I do so using np.flip()

Comparing times on the picture of me, my convolution algorithm averages around 1.75s across a 9x9 box filter, the Dx filter, and a 5x5 gaussian filter. This pales in comparison to scipy's convolve which does the same thing in milliseconds.

1.2 Finite Difference Operator

The Dx filter highlights the vertical edges while the Dy filter highlights the horizontal ones. The gradient magnitude squares and adds the values at each pixel together, like taking the hypotenuse of the two. It shows the overall edges of the image.

1.3 Derivative of Gaussian (DoG) Filter

I built the gaussian by first making a 1d gaussian, then taking the outer product with itself. This made the 2d gaussian kernel! With that, I can smooth out the gradient magnitude and then binarize it for a cleaner result.

Compared to the previous result, this result below is much better. Barely any grass / trash is picked up at all, and just the lines overall look a lot better. The DoG filters are made by convoluting the derivative filters with the gaussian. Part 2: Fun with Frequencies

2.1 Image "Sharpening"

The unsharp mask works by basically subtracting low frequencies from the image to get a sharper high frequency image. In more depth, we take the original image, add the image * a sharpening factor, and subtract a blurred image * that same sharpening factor

As alpha increases the image starts to look a lot sharper, the colors as well start to look a little brighter around edges, and overall it looks more intense. I ended up deciding that alpha = 2.0 is the best because that's the point at which my friends a couple meters away could tell the difference.

2.2 Hybrid Images

A hybrid image works by applying a low pass filter and a high pass filter on two images and stacking them on top of one another. From far away, the low frequencies will stand out more because high frequencies are less discernable the further you are. However, when you get closer the high pass frequencies start appearing and start to dominate the image. This creates a hybrid image that appears as two.

2.3 Gaussian and Laplacian Stacks

A gaussian stack is the original image repeatedly blurred. The laplacian stack is made by blurring the current level and subtracting the next blurred level. This creates a frequency band. You can get back the original image by adding the laplacian stack together + the final gaussian layer. I decided on 5 layers for my implementation.

2.4 Multiresolution Blending

Multiresolution blending works by building a Laplacian stack of both images. You can do this by applying the gaussian and then subtracting the next level gaussian (blurred again) to sort of create a frequency band. We can implement a mask over each frequency band in this way: mask * laplacian_a and inverted mask * laplacian_b. Once we have our masked images at each level, we can add them all together to get a blended image. By applying the mask at each step, we're applying a mask at each frequency level making the transition smooth for the whole image compared to a simple clean cut across. Also for the mask, make sure to have a associated gaussian stack since we want the frequencies to match.
